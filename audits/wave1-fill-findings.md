The phase-host cost calculation makes the refinement's adequacy explicit. Let `r` be the number of source steps already simulated, `p` its query-head displacement, `z` its query word, and `tCall` the clean-call duration. Preparation plus its surrounding redirects costs at most `natAbs p + 5`; copy, the one R2 handover, the call and service redirect cost `2·length(z)+4+tCall`; restoration plus the answer redirect costs `natAbs p+4`. Their sum is the claimed `2·natAbs p+2·length(z)+tCall+13`. The public radius/query-length bounds give `natAbs p ≤ r` and `length(z) ≤ r` for the initially blank query tape. If the chosen install call has coefficient `a` and duration at most `a·(τ(length(z))+length(z)+2)`, the query segment is bounded by `(a+4)·r+2a+13+a·τ(length(z))`. The delivered larger coefficient `2a+13` absorbs that bound and initialization in the global quadratic ledger. No replacement of `τ(length(z))` by `τ(r)` is used.
