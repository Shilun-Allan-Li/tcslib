## Priority question CH7-Q1 — exact rational counting

**The class conclusions are not weakened by the change of tail method.** `randProb m A` is the exact fraction of the `2^m` words in `{0,1}^m` satisfying `A`; at `m=0` its sample space is the singleton empty word. `blockCount q K B` evaluates `B` on `K` disjoint consecutive `q`-bit blocks. Its statement `randProb_blockCount` gives the exact binomial mass with success parameter `s = randProb q (B = true)`. `randProb_tail_le` then states, for `s ≤ 1/2−ε` and `ε≥0`,

\[
\Pr[\text{at least half of the }K\text{ blocks are bad}]
\le 2(1-4\varepsilon^2)^{\lfloor K/2\rfloor}.
\]

The leading `2` and floor are absorbed by the explicit repetition counts. `weakAdv c n = min(1/6,(n+1)^{-c})` is positive and at least `(n+1)^{-c}/6`. With `K=56(n+1)^{2c+d}`, `weakAdv_tail_bound` reaches error at most `2^{-((n+1)^d+1)}`. Thus `bpp_error_reduction` takes `InBPPWeak c` to `InBPPStrong d` for every natural `d`, and the latter is at least `3/4` correct even at `n=0`. The book's [Theorem 7.10](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/book.pdf) asks for error `2^{-n^d}` for every positive constant `d`; the Lean target is stronger for each natural `d`. A larger natural exponent covers any positive real exponent at positive lengths. The code does not state that real-parameter corollary, but it loses no asymptotic error-reduction conclusion.

The book's literal `1/2+n^{-c}` premise for [Lemma 7.9](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/book.pdf) exceeds `1` at `n=1` and is undefined at `n=0`. The capped `weakAdv` is an explicit small-length repair. It admits every ordinary BPP verifier because `weakAdv≤1/6`; its positive inverse-polynomial lower bound makes majority amplification work in the other direction. The Lean lemma is therefore a meaningful, slightly stronger robustness claim under this repaired definition, not a silent weakening.

For [Theorem 7.17](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/book.pdf), the `d=1` amplified failure bound is `2^{-(n+2)}` per input. There are `2^n` inputs of length `n`; their union has mass at most `1/4`, leaving a seed good for every input, including `n=0`. `VerifierHasCircuits` supplies a polynomial-size circuit for that fixed seed. For [Theorem 7.18](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/book.pdf), `amplify_concrete` supplies error at most `2^{-T}` for an explicit polynomial `T`; `shift_count_lt_two_pow` supplies the strict shift-count inequality needed to separate small and large acceptance sets. The XOR-shift and `∃u∀v` statements retain the book's two-alternation conclusion. These are mathematical implications of the ℚ estimates; the abstract Lean theorems require named efficiency closures. The polynomial-time versions still inherit the three open closure admissions (`ClosedUnderMajority`, `ClosedUnderAny`, `ClosedUnderShiftOr`), so they are not yet independent kernel proofs of the machine theorems.

At the adversarial corner `n=c=d=0`, the weak advantage is `1/6`, the repetition count is `56`, the target failure is `1/4`, and the rational tail estimate is about `0.07392`; the exact binomial bad-majority probability at per-trial error `1/3` is about `0.00724`. The inequalities do not collapse at zero length.

## Other specific questions, in pack order

**CH7-Q2.** The abort-form `InZPP` never outputs a wrong answer on sampled seeds and has abort mass ≤`1/2`. The race theorem matches `RP∩coRP` within that model. Expected-time equivalence needs the usual truncation and repeat-until-success bridge; see finding 2. No counterexample to the abort-form theorem arose.

**CH7-Q3.** `walk_visits_concentration` uses a uniform start, `k+1` visited vertices, exact density `β=|B|/n`, and the negative exponent `−(1−λ)δ²(k+1)/60`. The book's printed exponent is positive in [Theorem 7.41](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/bppchap.pdf); the sign correction is necessary. A [stronger expander-walk concentration theorem](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2006/058/download/) has denominator `36` for regular undirected walks. Its matrix argument uses symmetric stochasticity and contraction perpendicular to constants, so extension to the Lean weighted model is an inference from that proof. For `λ<1`, it gives the strict Lean `<` after weakening `36` to `60`; for `λ=1`, the Lean right side is `2`, hence strict `<` follows from probability ≤`1`. The remaining `sorry` is true as stated, but its short sketch does not itself establish the numerical constant; it identifies the external proof route. The book also omits this proof.

**CH7-Q4.** `polyTimeModel.Eff M` means the sets where `M x r` is `some true` and `some false`, encoded as `Turing.pairEncode x r`, each have `P` deciders. For `boolVerifier M` this determines both answers on every valid pair. `EffTwoWitness` uses a `P` truth set on `pairEncode (pairEncode x u) v`. The stated `inSigma2_polyTimeModel_iff` identifies this exact-length certificate form with `SigmaP 2`. The named closures are the operations the book tacitly performs; the three still sorried closures appear mathematically valid as polynomially many calls on polynomially sized strings. The shift sketch needs the correction in finding 1.

**CH7-Q5.** `pairWireList n r` routes input bit `j` to both coordinates `2j,2j+1`, followed by the constants `false,true` and the bits of `r`; `pairWiring` is this list indexed by `Fin`. Thus `pairEncodeInput r v = Turing.pairEncode (List.ofFn v) r`, exactly the circuit acceptance correspondence used by `polyTimeModel_verifierHasCircuits`. `C.pairEncode r` buffers the old inputs, and the stated size is `n+C.size`. At `n=0,r=[]`, the encoded word is `[false,true]` and the resulting circuit has no free input; at `r=[]` and `n>0`, the doubled input still remains. No coordinate loss was found.

**CH7-Q6.** `PrefixByLength.take/drop` and `Randomized.takePrefixByLen/dropPrefixByLen` have identical defining expressions: take/drop `|pairFstD z|` bits from `pairSndD z`. On a malformed pair such as aligned `[true,false]`, both decoded defaults are `[]` and both results are `[]`. The counter program's malformed path emits nothing. `sliceTake/Drop` re-encode the decoded first component with the corresponding polynomial-length prefix/suffix; on malformed input they return a valid encoding of two empty components, which is harmless for the valid-pair bridge. With a valid `pairEncode [] r`, take is `[]` and drop is `r`.

**CH7-Q7.** `shiftInput t s` changes only the input cursor, from `s.pos` to `t+s.pos`. `run_shiftInput` compares a suffix execution to a whole-input execution after prepending a consumed prefix. `Goes.prepend_input` shifts both endpoint positions by the prefix length while preserving registers, output, and time. With a two-bit prefix and a one-bit suffix, suffix positions `0,1` correspond to full positions `2,3`; the empty prefix is the identity. No sign or endpoint offset error appeared.

**Remaining Tier A statements.** `lambda_nonneg`/`lambda_le_one` bound the complement-of-constants operator norm in `[0,1]`; the mixing theorem is the book's normalized `|sᵀAt−|S||T|/n|≤λ√(|S||T|)`. The decomposition lemma permits `λ=0`, and the walk theorem's `k` transitions and `k+1` visited vertices shift the book's exponent `k−1` to `k`. Schwartz–Zippel bounds the fraction of nonzeros of a nonzero degree-`d` polynomial on a nonempty finite sampling set below by `1−d/|S|`; with zero variables it reduces to a nonzero constant, and with a singleton set its lower bound truncates to zero when the polynomial vanishes there. `ErrorReduction` separately states the valid analytic Hoeffding estimates `2e^{-2δ²k}` for a two-sided Bernoulli average and `e^{-2ε²k}` for a bad majority, with `k>0`; these are not the class-level ℚ argument. The book's printed [Corollary 7.11](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/book.pdf) needs repair: for one fair coin and `δ=0.4`, its absolute-deviation event has probability `1`, while its printed exponential right side is below `1`. The module's different two-sided bound avoids that counterexample.

## Adversarial cases and the four open statements

* **Zero length and empty randomness.** `polyLen a k 0=a`; if `a=0`, `randProb` samples only `[]`. `InBPPStrong` still demands success ≥`3/4`. The fixed-randomness circuit for `n=0,r=[]` has zero free inputs and evaluates the separator constants. These are non-vacuous requirements.
* **Constant verifier.** `M≡true` witnesses the universal language, and `M≡false` the empty language, but neither witnesses the opposite language under `InBPP`. Race, majority, OR, and shifted OR behave with their stated priorities. `ClosedUnder*` says only that these functions are efficient; an arbitrary zero repetition count does not falsely imply correctness because the amplification theorems choose positive counts.
* **Degenerate graph.** With `n=1`, `lambda=0` and the visitation deviation event is empty. A two-vertex identity or swap transition has `lambda=1`, so the Chernoff right side is `2` and the statement is true but uninformative. At `n=0`, the stochastic matrix facts degenerate as expected; walk PMFs require `[NeZero n]`.
* **Malformed pair and shifted cursor.** An aligned forbidden `10` defaults both decoded components to `[]` and the prefix routines return `[]`. A two-bit consumed prefix maps suffix cursor `0` to whole-input cursor `2`, and suffix EOF cursor `1` to whole-input cursor `3`.

The four `sorry` statements are true as stated on this review. `walk_visits_concentration` is supported by the stronger published expander bound described above. `polyTimeModel_closedUnderMajority` and `...closedUnderAny` need polynomially many calls to a `P` decider, extraction of polynomial-length blocks, and a polynomial counter/flag. `...closedUnderShiftOr` needs the same loop on nested-pair decoded inputs, polynomial-length shift blocks, truncating XOR, and OR aggregation. None needs an extra semantic hypothesis. Their implementations remain open, so this audit does not convert them into proved Lean theorems.
