## Proof review

| Declaration | Check |
|---|---|
| `hash_difference_probability` | Distinct bit words give a differing coordinate with residue difference $1$ or $-1$. Both are units for every allowed modulus, including composite moduli and modulus two. `piComm` and `funSplitAt` give a bijection to the other columns and the pivot column. The matrix-product difference splits into the pivot column scaled by that unit plus the sum from the other columns. With the other columns fixed, scaling and translating is a permutation, so each target has mass $1/q^d$. Averaging this constant mass over the nonempty other-column space preserves it. The output space has one element when $d=0$. The distinct-input hypothesis rules out zero columns. [Universality.lean](https://github.com/Shilun-Allan-Li/tcslib/blob/8c6b23f5b69260acb66cc99e322a81c4c79f51bb/TCSlib/Cryptography/Hashing/SIS/Universality.lean#L53-L102) |
| `leftover_error_le` | Casting the width bound to the reals gives $q^d\le 2^{dw}$. The exponent budget yields $q^d2^{2\kappa}\le 2^{dw+2\kappa}\le 2^m$. Both denominator powers are positive, so $q^d/2^m\le (2^{-\kappa})^2$. The target before the factor of $1/2$ is nonnegative, which justifies the square-root comparison. The argument includes $\kappa=0$ and $d=0$. [Universality.lean](https://github.com/Shilun-Allan-Li/tcslib/blob/8c6b23f5b69260acb66cc99e322a81c4c79f51bb/TCSlib/Cryptography/Hashing/SIS/Universality.lean#L160-L181) |
