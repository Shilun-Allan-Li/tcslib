### 2. New public declarations

Each item gives the independent signature restatement before the comparison with its docstring and source.

1. **`inner_mulVec_of_mem_unitaryGroup` (`Register.lean:191`).** Applying the same finite unitary matrix to two arbitrary complex vectors preserves their Hermitian pairing, with conjugation in the first argument. This follows from `(Ua)†(Ub) = a†(U†U)b = a†b`. The docstring matches de Wolf Appendix A.4. The lemma belongs with the coordinate identities for kets. Mathlib’s `LinearIsometry.inner_map_map` expresses the same principle for a packaged isometry, while this theorem supplies the raw matrix formula.
2. **`regAppendEquiv_symm_apply` (`Register.lean:140`).** Splitting a register returns its restrictions to the first `k` positions and last `m` positions. Every index lies in exactly one block, so concatenating the two restrictions recovers the original register, including when either block is empty. The docstring matches. Mathlib’s `Fin.appendEquiv` has the same inverse formula, and the TCSlib wrapper exposes the equation for the existing `regAppendEquiv` API beside its definition. [Pinned Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/029db123ddaa7f8fd0d18cea3b1b33bf84dacd1e/Mathlib/Logic/Equiv/Fin/Basic.lean#L404-L411)
3. **`inv_sqrt_two_mul_self` (`Gates.lean:214`).** The inverse of the complex cast of the real square root of two, multiplied by itself, equals `1/2`. The product is the inverse of the complex cast of `√2 · √2 = 2`. The docstring matches, and the lemma belongs with the Hadamard normalization in `Gates.lean`. It specializes standard Mathlib identities into a form used by Gates and Protocols.
4. **`applyOn_mulVec_apply` (`ApplyOn.lean:152`).** The output amplitude at `y` is the sum over local input assignments `u`, with each term reading the input vector at the register formed by writing `u` on `w` and keeping `y` elsewhere. Off-wire disagreement kills the corresponding matrix entry, and injectivity gives a unique compatible input for each `u`. The docstring explains `Function.extend` and the theorem belongs in `ApplyOn.lean`. Its coordinate formula generalizes the unitary-gate placement described by de Wolf §2.1.2.
5. **`applyOn_wire_mulVec_apply` (`ApplyOn.lean:406`).** The placed one-wire operator gives a two-term sum over input bits `a`, reading the input amplitude at `Function.update y i a`. Every `Reg 1` is constant at its value on `0`, so the constant row and column indices are the natural form for the current matrix API. The statement belongs beside `wire` in `ApplyOn.lean`. The docstring accurately gives the formula, subject to the input-bit wording and source scope in finding 4.
6. **`applyOn_permMat_mulVec_apply` (`ApplyOn.lean:428`).** A placed permutation reads the amplitude whose local assignment is the inverse permutation of `y ∘ w`, with other wires copied from `y`. The only surviving term has `σ(u) = y ∘ w`, fixing the inverse direction even for a non-involutive permutation. The docstring explains the reconstruction and the result belongs in `ApplyOn.lean`. Mathlib’s unplaced `Matrix.permMatrix_mulVec` uses the opposite convention and does not cover arbitrary wire placement. [Pinned Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/029db123ddaa7f8fd0d18cea3b1b33bf84dacd1e/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Permutation.lean#L64-L67)

### 3. Duplication ledger

The screen reproduced 28 directional rows. Of the 21 E1 rows, 18 are shared idioms across different results. The other three are the misattributed Measure match and the two Hadamard directions. Removing the example in a scratch copy removes exactly the misattributed row. The seven older rows are confined to two unchanged quantum error-correction files. They cover left/right bilinearity, related Hamming definitions, and pre-existing complement-cardinality arithmetic in Singleton. No row crosses from the E1 files to the older files.

Both recorded removals are confirmed against pre-cleanup `f93c7ee6`. `applyOn_mul` now uses `applyOn_mulVec_apply` on a column, replacing its separate filtered-sum bijection. `permMat_mulVec_apply` now uses `applyOn_permMat_mulVec_apply` through `applyOn_refl`, replacing its separate single-term calculation. Two smaller reuse opportunities are the overlapping `extend`-to-`update` argument in the one-wire proofs and the parallel short involution-power arguments in Protocols.

### 4. Proof sketches

All 16 rewritten sketches describe their implemented mathematical routes. Register uses the complex dot product. Gates uses permutation indicators, four-entry identities, and a finite Toffoli slice. ApplyOn uses column reduction and two instances of the placed-permutation basis action. Measure uses projection commutation and unitary norm preservation. Protocols uses register splitting, a two-term sum with eight cases for teleportation, inverse-power cancellation, and one sixteen-case computation for superdense coding. The remaining sketches were checked at the level of mathematical routes. Findings 3 and 5 record the discrepancies.

### 5. Private helpers

The 28 private helpers break down as 1 Register, 4 Gates, 9 ApplyOn, 2 Measure, and 12 Protocols. No further promotion is needed for the current consumers. Placed-permutation basis action, disjoint multiplication, and projection commutation are candidates for shared APIs when another file needs them. All six public additions have distinct uses or expose equations of existing public abstractions.
