/-!
# Buffer tapes of composite words

Cell identities for the buffer tape `Turing.FinTM.bufferTape w`, which holds the word `w` from
cell `0` and is blank everywhere else, when `w` is built from smaller words: a suffix read at an
offset, a middle word's letters, a prefix or a suffix of the word, the blank cells off the word,
and the cells of a unary word with its terminator. A machine's contract can then read the fields
of a serialized layout, such as a pair encoding followed by a unary state and a table, cell by
cell. Every proof reads `bufferTape` only through its published equations.

## Main results

* `Turing.FinTM.bufferTape_append_shift` — past a prefix `u`, the buffer of `u ++ w` reads `w`'s
  buffer.
* `Turing.FinTM.bufferTape_append_mid` — the letters of the middle word of `u ++ w ++ v`.
* `Turing.FinTM.bufferTape_drop`, `Turing.FinTM.bufferTape_take` — the buffer of a suffix or a
  prefix of a word.
* `Turing.FinTM.bufferTape_eq_none_of_not_mem` — every cell off the word is blank.
* `Turing.FinTM.bufferTape_unary` — the cells of the unary word `1^q 0`, its terminator
  included.

## References

* [AB09] S. Arora, B. Barak, *Computational Complexity: A Modern Approach*,
  Cambridge University Press, 2009. (§1.2: tapes holding words; §1.4: encodings of machines as
  strings, whose fields these identities read.)
-/

namespace Turing.FinTM
section BufferWordsSeal
attribute [local irreducible] Turing.FinTM.bufferTape

example (w : List Bool) : bufferTape w (-1) = none := rfl

example (w : List Bool) (z : ℤ) : bufferTape w z = if 0 ≤ z then w[z.toNat]? else none := rfl

end BufferWordsSeal
end Turing.FinTM
