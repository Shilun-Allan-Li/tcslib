**Adversarial checks.** These are mathematical source checks, not newly kernel-checked tests.

1. **Segments need not be first exits.** Take a stationary, silent machine idling at a live state `a`, distinct from both forbidden verdicts. Its length-two segment satisfies `RecordRun`. A first-exit contract with that endpoint must contain `some a` among its exits, contradicting its cut at time zero. A returning contract of duration two contradicts its cut at time one. The planned segment layer avoids both extra requirements. The same example away from a forbidden anchor satisfies `f2_splitSafe`, including its endpoint check.
2. **Return-or-escape is different.** A positive run starting at `a` and ending either at `a` or at a distinct escape `e` cannot use an ordinary contract with exits `{some a, some e}`: its time-zero cut fails. A returning contract excludes the escaping endpoint and does not alone exclude an earlier escape on a returning run. The planned round layer explicitly retains both positive cuts.
3. **Joint witnesses cannot be replaced by intervals.** The head vectors `(0,0)` and `(1,1)` satisfy equality of coordinates. Their coordinate intervals also admit `(0,1)`, which violates that equality. The new trajectory layer must preserve the joint predicate, including the shared round/time witness in the actual counter-loop conclusion.
4. **`counterSpan` has a real zero-time case.** Set its duration, counter position and width to zero, put the counter head at zero, and choose the permitted-vector predicate constantly true. The strict cut is empty; the only envelope check is `0 ∈ [-1,0]`; the vector condition is true. Thus `counterSpan` holds, whereas a positive round cannot have this duration. The attached `counter_rounds` proof also constructs a zero-time span in its base case.
