# All-input simulation bounds

**Claim.** The single-tape simulator computes the encoded output of the machine on the retracted input within the stated quadratic time bound, and its space use at every time is bounded by the stated linear expression.

**Proof.** Let $y$ be the word obtained by retracting each symbol of $x$. By the machine's time guarantee, it halts on $y$ by time $T(|y|)$. Choose its first halting time $\tau$; then $\tau\le T(|y|)$, and every earlier configuration is nonhalting. The all-times simulation lemma (`dg_simulate`) applied through time $\tau$ supplies an encoded simulator configuration reached at some time $u\le\mathrm{sweepTime}(k,\tau)$, with the simulated machine's configuration represented by a window of width $n$ and the simulator head confined to the corresponding span. Since the simulated machine has halted at $\tau$, its output there is $f(y)$ and remains unchanged at every later time (`runFrom_output_eq_of_halt`). Thus the simulator configuration at time $u$ is halting and has output equal to the encoding of $f(y)$. The equivalence between computation and having the required halting state and output (`computesInTime_iff`) gives computation by time $u$; monotonicity (`ComputesInTime.mono`) extends this to the claimed bound. Indeed, $u\le\mathrm{sweepTime}(k,\tau)\le(9k+6)(\tau+1)^2\le(9k+6)(T(|x|)+1)^2$, using $|y|=|x|$ and the sweep-time estimate (`sweepTime_le`). For space, the simulator head remains within the simulated window's span through time $u$. After $u$, the simulator is halted and hence stays in its final configuration, by `runFrom_add` and `runFrom_of_halt`. Therefore, at every time its visited positions lie in the integer interval from the window's left endpoint to its right endpoint. This interval has $nk+2$ positions, so the single tape uses at most $nk+2$ space. The window-space bound (`dgWindow_space`) and the assumed space bound give $n\le S(|y|)=S(|x|)$. Hence $nk+2\le(k+2)(S(|x|)+1)\le(9k+6)(S(|x|)+1)$, proving the asserted uniform space bound.
