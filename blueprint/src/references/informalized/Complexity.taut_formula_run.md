# Evaluation of a Serialized Formula

**Claim.** For a CNF formula whose literal indices lie within the assignment word, the verifier evaluates its serialization in at most three times the serialization length plus one steps, halting with the bit that negates the formula’s DNF value under the assignment.

**Proof.** Proceed by induction on the formula. For the empty formula, its serialization is the single false bit. The verifier reads the encoded input marker and reaches the verdict state for the empty disjunction; one more step halts and outputs the required bit. This takes three steps, within the stated bound. For a formula with first clause $C$ and remaining formula $\varphi$, split the input prefix into the doubled encoding of the formula marker and the doubled encoding of the serialized clause. The doubling-length fact (`taut_double_length`) and pair-encoding decomposition (`taut_pair_append`) give the corresponding input positions and leave the encoding of $\varphi$ available after the clause. The two-step evaluation transition (`taut_eval_double`) enters clause evaluation with accumulator true. By the clause evaluation result (`taut_clause_run`), within three times the clause serialization length the verifier has evaluated $C$ and either entered the false verdict state if every literal in $C$ matches the assignment, or returned to formula evaluation at the start of the remaining formula. In the first case, one further step halts with false, which is the negation of the DNF value because this clause already makes the DNF true. In the second case, apply the induction hypothesis to the remaining formula and concatenate the runs using `runFrom_add`. The resulting output is the negation of the remaining formula’s DNF value; since the first clause is false, this is also the negation of the full formula’s DNF value. The time bounds in both branches follow from the decomposition of the serialization length and the induction bound. The input-position bounds follow from the pair-encoding length formula (`universal_pair_length`).
