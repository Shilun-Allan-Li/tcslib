# Walk reachability probability

**Claim.** For a symmetric encoded adjacency relation and the stated space and input-length bounds, the program started at $w0$ terminates with bounded registers and accepts with probability equal to the probability that a uniformly random padded-graph walk of length $k$ from $v$ reaches the target.

**Proof.** We induct on the walk length $k$. The degree of the padded graph is positive and at least $n$, while the register bound follows from the stated size estimate together with the upper bound on the padded degree. The graph's hit probability obeys the usual first-step recurrence: at length zero it is $1$ exactly at the target; at length $k+1$ it is $1$ if the starting vertex is the target, and otherwise is the average, over all edge slots, of the length-$k$ hit probabilities from the corresponding neighbors (the facts `hitProb_zero` and `hitProb_succ`).

For $k=0$, the program compares the current vertex with the target. Equality takes the accepting branch, and inequality takes the rejecting branch, giving exactly the length-zero hit probability. For the successor case, the same comparison handles an already-hit target immediately. Otherwise the program clears its random-index register and copies the walk data needed for the next step. Fix a slot index $j$ and consider the continuation after that slot is chosen. Its acceptance probability is the length-$k$ hit probability from the neighbor selected by slot $j$, by the induction hypothesis; the program's counter increment accounts for the additional step.

The program's slot-selection routine samples uniformly from the padded degree: `ups_coin` identifies its acceptance probability as the average of the continuations over the slot range, and `sum_slots` rewrites this average as the graph's average over all edge slots. To identify each continuation, the program first handles indices $j<n$ by translating the row-major address of the edge to the appropriate input position (`rowMajor_lt`, `ups_index`). The input-bit oracle reports whether the encoded edge is present (`pathOr_bit`). If present, the neighbor is the indexed vertex; if absent, padding makes the neighbor the current vertex. For $j\ge n$, the slot is padding by definition and again leaves the vertex unchanged. In each case the program sets its current-vertex register to that neighbor and invokes the induction hypothesis. Thus the slot average agrees with the first-step recurrence, completing the induction. The register-bound transfer and the deterministic instruction rules ensure these continuations have the required semantics throughout.
