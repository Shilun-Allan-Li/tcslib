# Correctness and Bounds for Tree Emission

**Claim.** For an AND or OR kind and a valid list of vertices, tree emission returns an acyclic extension with a valid root whose value is the single gate applied to the listed values. Its size and depth obey the stated bounds, and every appended gate has the required kind, distinct inputs, and at most two inputs.

**Proof.** Proceed by induction on the number of listed vertices, treating the empty and singleton cases directly. For the empty list, emission appends one gate with no inputs. Its value is the empty-input value of the chosen gate kind, its depth is one, and the size is the specified empty-list cost. Appending a gate whose inputs are valid preserves acyclicity (`BoolCircuit.GatesAcyclic.snoc`). For a singleton, emission returns its existing vertex; the singleton evaluation identity (`BoolCircuit.DAGGate.eval_singleton`) gives the value claim, and no gates are added.

For a list of at least two vertices, split it into the first part of length $\lfloor (|vs|+1)/2\rfloor$ and the remaining part. Recursively emit a tree for each part, first on the original circuit and then on the circuit extended by the first emission. The split parts are shorter, so both induction hypotheses apply. The original vertices remain valid in the extended circuit because the first result extends the original gate list. Write $r_1,r_2$ for the two resulting roots. Append one gate of kind $k$ whose arguments are the deduplicated list $[r_1,r_2]$. Both roots precede this final gate, so the acyclicity condition for the entire extension follows by appending it to the second recursive circuit.

The final gate's root is within the resulting vertex range. Its value is the operation of kind $k$ on the two recursive root values: deduplication does not change an AND or OR evaluation (`BoolCircuit.DAGGate.eval_dedup`), and evaluation on two inputs gives the corresponding binary operation (`BoolCircuit.DAGGate.eval_pair`). Each recursive root computes the gate on its own part. The first root's value is preserved through the second extension; for the second root, the original vertices' values are preserved through the first extension. Thus the two values are the operations on the two parts, and combining them gives the operation on the original list (`BoolCircuit.DAGGate.eval_append`). This argument uses that $k$ is AND or OR, so concatenation splits evaluation into the binary operation.

For the length bound, add the two recursive length bounds and the one final gate. The split sizes sum to $|vs|$; the empty-part convention in the cost and the nonempty cost $s-1$ give the claimed total bound. For depth, the final gate has depth one plus the maximum of the two root depths. The induction bounds control each recursive depth by its part's logarithmic height plus the maximum source depth in that part. Each part's maximum is bounded by the maximum over the full list (`BoolCircuit.foldr_max_le₂`, `BoolCircuit.le_foldr_max₂`), and the two part lengths are at most $\lceil |vs|/2\rceil$. Monotonicity and the halving identity for the base-two ceiling logarithm give the stated depth bound. Finally, the appended gates are precisely the two recursive extensions and the last gate; the induction hypotheses handle the recursive gates, while the final gate has kind $k$, deduplicated (hence distinct) inputs, and at most two inputs.
