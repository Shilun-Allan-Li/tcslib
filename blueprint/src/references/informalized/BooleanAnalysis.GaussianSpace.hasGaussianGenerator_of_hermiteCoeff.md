# Hermite coefficients identify the Gaussian generator

**Claim.** If $f$ and $g$ are square-integrable under product standard Gaussian measure and every Hermite coefficient of $g$ is the total degree of its multi-index times the corresponding coefficient of $f$, then $f$ has Gaussian generator $g$: the Gaussian noise quotient converges to $g$ in $L^2$ as $t\to0^+$.

**Proof.** First, the squares of the Hermite coefficients of $g$ are summable because $g$ is square-integrable (\texttt{hermiteCoeff\_sq\_summable}). The coefficient hypothesis therefore shows that the squared sequence of degree-weighted coefficients of $f$ is summable as well. Apply the convergence theorem for the Gaussian-generator multiplier error (\texttt{gaussianGeneratorMultiplier\_error\_tsum\_tendsto}) to this sequence: as $t$ tends to zero from above, the sum of squared errors between the multiplier at degree $|\alpha|$ and $|\alpha|$, weighted by the squared coefficient of $f$, tends to zero. Taking square roots and converting the real limit to an extended-real limit gives convergence of the corresponding $L^2$ norms to zero. For each positive $t$, the Hermite coefficient of the Gaussian noise quotient is the multiplier at degree $|\alpha|$ times the coefficient of $f$ (\texttt{hermiteCoeff\_gaussianGeneratorQuotient}). Subtracting $g$ subtracts its coefficient, by linearity of Hermite coefficients (\texttt{hermiteCoeff\_sub}); the assumed identity for the coefficients of $g$ leaves exactly the multiplier error just estimated. The $L^2$ norm equals the square root of the sum of squared Hermite coefficients (\texttt{eLpNorm\_two\_eq\_sqrt\_hermiteCoeff}), and the quotient is square-integrable for positive $t$ (\texttt{memLp\_gaussianGeneratorQuotient}). Hence the required $L^2$ convergence follows, together with the given square-integrability of $f$ and $g$.
