# Invariance Under Mixing

**Claim.** If two samples yield the same achievable value $z$ of $Z$, then mixing Alice's data from the first with Bob's data from the second again yields $z$.

**Proof.** Let $\omega_X$ and $\omega_Y$ yield $z$. They have the same special coordinate, the same Alice window before it, and the same Bob window after it, by the corresponding same-$Z$ facts (`specialCoordinate_eq_of_same_zVariable`, `xBeforeSpecial_eq_of_same_zVariable`, and `yAfterSpecial_eq_of_same_zVariable`). The input of their mix is therefore $(X(\omega_X),Y(\omega_Y))$ (`input_mix`). The mixed-input rectangle lemma says this cross-pair lies in the transcript rectangle associated with $z$ (`mixed_inputs_mem_transcript_of_zVariable_eq`). Membership in that rectangle forces its protocol transcript to be $z$'s transcript (`Deterministic.Protocol.transcript_eq_of_mem`).

It remains to identify the other three components. The mix retains the common special coordinate, which equals $z$'s recorded coordinate (`specialCoordinate_eq_of_zVariable_eq`). Its Alice window is the first sample's window (`xBeforeSpecial_mix`), hence equals $z.xBefore$ (`xBeforeSpecial_eq_of_zVariable_eq`). Its Bob window is the second sample's window (`yAfterSpecial_mix`), hence equals $z.yAfter$ (`yAfterSpecial_eq_of_zVariable_eq`). Thus all four components of the mixed sample's raw value agree with those of $z$. Equality of raw records (`RawZType.ext`) and then equality of the achievable subtype values give the claim.
