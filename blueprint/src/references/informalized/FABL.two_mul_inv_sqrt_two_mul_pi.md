# Gaussian normalization identity

**Claim.** The Gaussian normalization constants satisfy $2(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}=\sqrt{2/\pi}$.

**Proof.** Use the product and quotient rules for square roots to write $\sqrt{2\pi}=\sqrt 2\,\sqrt\pi$ and $\sqrt{2/\pi}=\sqrt 2/\sqrt\pi$, which apply since $2$ and $\pi$ are nonnegative. Both square roots are nonzero. The claimed identity then reduces, after multiplying by the nonzero factors, to $2\sqrt\pi=2\sqrt\pi$; the square-root identity $\bigl(\sqrt2\bigr)^2=2$ supplies the corresponding algebraic simplification.
