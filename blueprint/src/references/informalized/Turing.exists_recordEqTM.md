# Machine for Comparing Records

**Claim.** For every natural number $k$ and finite decidable state types $Q$ and $T$, there exist a finite decidable state set, a deterministic two-work-tape machine, distinct yes and no states, an entry state, and a constant such that the machine decides whether the two records with exactly $k+3$ fields are equal, with the stated time, first-visit, head-displacement, and final-configuration guarantees.

**Proof.** The machine is built by a phase controller that compares corresponding fields from left to right. Write $L$ for the length of the joined first record and $B=12(L+1)$. The proof tracks each tape head by a field index: `fieldOffset_add_length_le` bounds these offsets by the record length, while `fieldOffset_succ` identifies the offset of the next field. The record hypotheses, together with `fieldsTape_field`, show that at each such offset the tape presents the field as a word; equality of the preceding field lists gives the corresponding word on the second tape. The auxiliary prefix and field identities in the proof make this information available inductively as the comparison advances.

For a current field, `compareTM_run_ofCfg` compares its two words and returns the equality verdict, with time bounded by twice the shorter word length plus a constant and with controlled head travel. If the field agrees, the machine uses two `skipTM_run_ofCfg` runs to move the two heads forward over the field and separator, then continues to the next field. If a mismatch occurs, or if the last field agrees, the proof uses the backward-skip routine and the inductive restoration argument to bring the heads back to their starting field positions before entering the appropriate verdict state. The bounds on field offsets and the chosen value of $B$ pay for each comparison and the associated forward or backward traversals.

More precisely, induction on the number of fields remaining proves `hsolve`: the comparison at the current field either detects inequality and restores the heads to the no verdict, or establishes equality through the final field and restores them to the yes verdict. The helper `hrestore` supplies the restoration runs, and `hforward` supplies the two-head move to the next field when the current field agrees. `phaseHostTM_run` implements the successive routines within one finite-state machine; `recordRun_tagged` and `recordRun_trans` preserve the no-early-verdict and head-radius guarantees as these runs are joined. With $k+3$ fields, the resulting time is at most $(k+3)B+(k+2)B=12(2k+5)(L+1)$, and the radius $L+1$ is bounded by the same stated scale. Since the input records have equal field counts, the final verdict is yes exactly when the lists of fields are equal, and the final configuration differs from the initial one only in its state.
