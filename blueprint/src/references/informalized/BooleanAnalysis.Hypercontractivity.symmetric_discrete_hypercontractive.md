# Hypercontractivity for symmetric discrete laws

**Claim.** Under the stated symmetry, finite-support, atom-mass, and exponent assumptions, $X$ is hypercontractive in both asserted exponent pairs, with radius $\lambda^{1/2-1/q}/\sqrt{q-1}$.

**Proof.** Set $A=\lambda^{1/2-1/q}$, $r=A/\sqrt{q-1}$, and $N=\|X\|_2$. The atom lower bound gives membership of $X$ in both required spaces (\texttt{BooleanAnalysis.Hypercontractivity.HasAtomLowerBound.memLp}). Symmetry implies that $X$ has mean zero. The assumptions on $q$ and $\lambda$ show $0<A\le1$, $\sqrt{q-1}>1$, and hence $0\le r<1$; also $2\le q$. We first prove the forward hypercontractive estimate. If $N=0$, the vanishing $L^2$ norm implies $X=0$ almost everywhere. Every affine perturbation is then almost everywhere constant, so the required norm inequality holds directly. Otherwise normalize by setting $Y=N^{-1}X$. This preserves symmetry, and the scaling property of the norms gives $\|Y\|_2=1$. Monotonicity of norms in the exponent gives $C:=\|Y\|_q\ge1$. Apply the symmetric unit-second-norm hypercontractivity theorem (\texttt{BooleanAnalysis.Hypercontractivity.symmetric\_hypercontractive}) to obtain radius $1/(C\sqrt{q-1})$ for $Y$. Rescaling transfers this estimate to $X$ at the same radius. The discrete norm bound (\texttt{BooleanAnalysis.Hypercontractivity.discrete\_lp\_norm\_le}) gives $\|X\|_q\le A^{-1}N$, and therefore $C\le A^{-1}$. Consequently $AC\le1$, which says $r\le1/(C\sqrt{q-1})$. Decreasing the radius preserves hypercontractivity when the variable has mean zero (\texttt{BooleanAnalysis.Hypercontractivity.IsHypercontractive.mono\_radius}), so the forward estimate holds at $r$. Finally, the conjugacy theorem for mean-zero variables converts this estimate into the asserted estimate with exponents $q/(q-1)$ and $2$ (\texttt{BooleanAnalysis.Hypercontractivity.IsHypercontractive.conjugate}).
