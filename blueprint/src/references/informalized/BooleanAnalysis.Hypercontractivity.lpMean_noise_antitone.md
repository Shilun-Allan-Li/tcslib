# Monotonicity Under Increased Noise

**Claim.** If $q<1$ and $0\le\rho\le\sigma\le1$, then for every nonnegative $f$, $M_q(T_\rho f)\ge M_q(T_\sigma f)$.

**Proof.** The proof first establishes a one-step statement: for $0\le\tau\le1$ and any $r<1$, applying $T_\tau$ to a nonnegative function cannot decrease its extended power mean. The noise kernel is nonnegative, has row and column sums $1$, and expresses the noise operator as a kernel average (`noiseKernel_nonneg`, `noiseKernel_sum_left`, `noiseKernel_sum_right`, `noiseOp_eq_kernel_sum`). In particular, noise preserves nonnegativity (`noiseOp_nonneg`). If $0<r<1$, concavity of $t^r$ and the fact that each row of the kernel has total weight $1$ give $\mathbb E u^r\le\mathbb E(T_\tau u)^r$. The positive-exponent formula (`lpMean_of_pos`) and monotonicity of the outer power then prove the one-step claim. If $r\le0$ and $u$ has a zero, its mean is zero by definition, while the mean after noise is nonnegative. If $u$ is strictly positive, the positive diagonal kernel entry ensures that $T_\tau u$ is strictly positive too. For $r=0$, concavity of logarithm applied to each kernel average gives $\mathbb E\log u\le\mathbb E\log(T_\tau u)$, and exponentiating proves the claim. For $r<0$, the function $t^r$ is convex on positive reals, so Jensen gives the reverse moment inequality, $\mathbb E(T_\tau u)^r\le\mathbb E u^r$. Since the outer exponent $1/r$ is negative, it reverses this inequality and proves the desired comparison. To pass from one step to the requested parameters, if $\sigma=0$, then $\rho=0$ and the operators agree. Otherwise let $\tau=\rho/\sigma$, which lies in $[0,1]$. The noise operators compose by multiplying their parameters (`noiseOp_compose`), so $T_\tau(T_\sigma f)=T_\rho f$. Apply the one-step result to $T_\sigma f$, which remains nonnegative, to obtain $M_q(T_\rho f)\ge M_q(T_\sigma f)$.
