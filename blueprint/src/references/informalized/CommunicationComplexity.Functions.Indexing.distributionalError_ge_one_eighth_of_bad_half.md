# Many Bad Inputs Force Error

**Claim.** If at least half of the $n$-bit strings are bad, then the protocol's error probability under the uniform distribution on input pairs is at least $1/8$.

**Proof.** Let $B$ be the set of bad strings, and for each string $x$ let $g(x)$ count the coordinates on which the protocol's answer disagrees with $x$. The total number of incorrect pairs is the sum of these mismatch counts over all strings (CommunicationComplexity.Functions.Indexing.badPairSubtype_card_eq_sum). Dropping the nonnegative contributions from strings outside $B$ can only decrease this sum. For every $x\in B$, the bad-input distance bound gives Hamming distance at least $n/4$ from the answer vector generated by the message sent on $x$ (CommunicationComplexity.Functions.Indexing.badInput_implies_hammingDist_ge); the mismatch-count/Hamming-distance identity identifies this with $g(x)$ (CommunicationComplexity.Functions.Indexing.mismatchSubtype_card_eq_hammingDist). Thus the total number of incorrect pairs is at least $|B|n/4$, which, by the assumed lower bound on $|B|$, is at least $2^{n-1}n/4$. There are $2^n n$ input pairs in all. Under the uniform distribution, the error probability is the number of incorrect pairs divided by the total number of pairs (CommunicationComplexity.uniformOn_univ_measureReal_eq_card_filter). The resulting ratio is at least $(2^{n-1}n/4)/(2^n n)=1/8$, as required.
