# Linear and queried-coordinate scores

**Claim.** For a decision tree computing $f$, the product mean of $f$ times the full sum of standardized coordinate signs equals the product mean of $f$ times the sum over coordinates queried by the tree.

**Proof.** Proceed by induction on the tree. At a leaf, $f$ is constant. Each standardized coordinate sign has mean zero, so the full linear score has mean zero; the queried-coordinate score is also zero because the leaf makes no queries. Now suppose the root queries coordinate $k$ and has a child tree for each possible value $\omega$ at that coordinate. Fixing this coordinate in $f$ gives a function computed by the corresponding child: the child’s output is unchanged when inputs agree on its available coordinates, and the computing identity for the parent then gives the claim. For each $\omega$, write $f_\omega$ for this section, and split the full score into the standardized sign at $k$ plus the sum over all other coordinates. The score of the section has zero correlation with the sign at $k$, since the section ignores coordinate $k$ (\texttt{productMean_mul_standardizedSign_eq_zero_of_ignore}). Hence its product mean against the full score equals its mean against the sum over the other coordinates. The induction hypothesis identifies that latter mean with its mean against the child’s queried-coordinate score. To pass between the original input and these sections, apply the diagonal-section identity to both integrands; the remaining-coordinate sum is invariant under replacing coordinate $k$, and the child score is invariant there because $k$ is unavailable to the child. In each section, adding the fixed sign value at $k$ contributes that value times the mean of $f_\omega$ on both sides. Linearity of expectation then makes the section means equal, and averaging over $\omega$ proves the result.
