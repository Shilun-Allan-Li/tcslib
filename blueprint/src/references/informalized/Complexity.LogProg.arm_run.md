# Halting computation simulation

**Claim.** A halting register computation with the stated preconditions and register-length bounds is simulated by the compiled tape machine, which halts with the prescribed answer appended to the output and satisfies the head-position bounds and invariant along the way.

**Proof.** Induct on the number $N$ of abstract steps. The zero-step case is impossible because the initial register configuration has a state, whereas the hypothesis says the final configuration is halted. For the successor case, the time-zero assumptions provide the instruction precondition and the length bound on each register. They also imply $0\le W$ and, by monotonicity of binary-word length (`length_bits_mono`), the bound $|\mathrm{bits}(v(r))|\le W$ for every register. Consider the first instruction. For an ordinary instruction whose abstract step continues in state $l'$ with valuation $v'$, its corresponding simulation result supplies a finite tape run from the current encoding to the encoding of $(l',v')$, with the appropriate space bound. This covers increments, decrements, clearing, halving, zero and oddness tests, register equality, and the three input-word comparisons. The precondition supplies distinctness for the two registers in the equality test and validity of the input encoding for the input-word comparisons. Append the induction-hypothesis run from the successor encoding; the shifted hypotheses follow from `arun_succ`. The concatenation is justified by `rrun_add`, and `inv_append` combines the intermediate invariants and head bounds across the two runs. If the first abstract step halts, its answer must equal $b$, since halted register configurations remain fixed (`arun_halted`) and the final answer is $b$. The compiled instruction's halting simulation (`sim_ret`, or the rejecting branches of `sim_valP` and `sim_valQ`) then gives the required halt and output. Its intermediate configurations satisfy the invariant by `inv_of_mid`. For a call instruction, `sim_call` performs the first compiled transition to the branch selected by the oracle; the call precondition supplies the clean decider run needed for the initial call invariant. The induction hypothesis applies to the remaining abstract computation, and the same run concatenation gives the result. In every continuing case, the final head bounds come from the compiled simulation and its continuation; the output is preserved until the halting instruction appends $b$.
