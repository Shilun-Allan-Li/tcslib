# Mean Distance from a Second-Moment Bound

**Claim.** For the conditioned hard distribution, if the mean square of the distance between Alice’s conditional special-bit law and the uniform bit law is at most $\gamma^4$, then the mean of that distance is at most $\gamma^2$.

**Proof.** Let $f(\omega)$ be the distance at the value of $Z$ generated by $\omega$, and let $\mu$ be the doubly conditioned hard distribution. Since $\mu$ is a probability measure (\texttt{disjointSpecialYFalseMeasure_isProbabilityMeasure}), Jensen’s inequality for the convex function $x\mapsto x^2$ gives $(\int f\,d\mu)^2\leq\int f^2\,d\mu$. The hypothesis bounds the right side by $\gamma^4=(\gamma^2)^2$. Also $f$ is nonnegative at every sample, by nonnegativity of total variation distance (\texttt{xDistance_nonneg}); hence its integral is nonnegative. Since $\gamma^2\geq0$, comparison of the squares yields $\int f\,d\mu\leq\gamma^2$.
