# One-Step Simulation with Window Growth

**Claim.** Under the stated head-location, blank-boundary, and transition-flag assumptions, the simulator carries the encoding of the source configuration and its window to the encoding of the one-step successor and expanded window. It takes $\kappa_k(n,\mathit{left},\mathit{right})$ steps, while its work head remains between $z-\mathit{left}\,k$ and $z+1+(n+\mathit{right})k$.

**Proof.** Break the simulator run into four consecutive phases. First, `dg_collect` performs the forward sweep across the current window, collecting the symbols and head data and ending in the read phase at its right boundary; `dgSpan_mono` widens its head-position interval to the final bounds. The stored reads agree with the source heads because every head lies in the window. At the right boundary, the right-growth test agrees with the assumed `right` flag. If it is false, `dg_turn_without_growth` turns into the writing phase directly; if true, `dg_turn_with_growth` first creates the additional right guard row. In the latter case, `dg_new_left_row` identifies that guard with the actual next row, using the blankness at the old right boundary. In either branch, the dispatch span ends at the writing phase with the appropriate enlarged zone. Next, `dg_write` sweeps backward across that zone, applying the source action to the encoded cells and recording the head flags for the left boundary; `dg_sweep_reverse_prefix` bounds the head during every prefix of this sweep. Finally, the left-boundary test agrees with `left`. If it is false, `dg_end_without_growth` finishes directly; if true, `dg_end_with_growth` adds the new left row, identified with the successor configuration's row by `dg_new_left_row` and the blankness at the old left boundary. Combining the four spans with `dgSpan_add` gives the total cost `dgCost` and the stated endpoint. The interval bounds are preserved or widened at the phase boundaries by `dgSpan_mono`; the cost's four summands are exactly the lengths of the collection, dispatch, writing, and finalization phases.
