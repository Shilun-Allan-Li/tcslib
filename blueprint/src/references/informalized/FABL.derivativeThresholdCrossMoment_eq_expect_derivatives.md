# Cross moment identity for derivative thresholds

**Claim.** For a real-valued function $p$ on the sign cube and distinct coordinates $i,j$, the expected product of the two input signs and the two thresholded discrete derivatives equals the expected product of the crossed discrete derivatives of those threshold functions.

**Proof.** Write $g_i$ and $g_j$ for the real-valued thresholded derivatives in coordinates $i$ and $j$. Each is independent of the coordinate from which its derivative was formed. Since $i\ne j$, the sign in coordinate $j$ is independent of coordinate $i$; consequently its product with $g_i$ remains independent of coordinate $i$ ($\texttt{FABL.coordinateSign\_isCoordinateIndependent}$ and $\texttt{FABL.IsCoordinateIndependent.mul}$). Also, taking the discrete derivative in coordinate $i$ of $g_j$ preserves independence from coordinate $j$ ($\texttt{FABL.IsCoordinateIndependent.discreteDerivative\_of\_ne}$).\n\nFirst rearrange the integrand to group the sign in coordinate $i$ with $(\operatorname{sign}(x_j)g_i(x))g_j(x)$. Averaging a coordinate sign times a function is the same as averaging its discrete derivative in that coordinate ($\texttt{FABL.expect\_coordinateSign\_mul\_eq\_expect\_discreteDerivative}$). The discrete product rule then pulls out the factor independent of coordinate $i$, giving $\operatorname{sign}(x_j)g_i(x)\,D_i g_j(x)$ ($\texttt{FABL.discreteDerivative\_mul\_of\_left\_independent}$). Regroup as the sign in coordinate $j$ times $g_i(x)D_i g_j(x)$ and apply the same averaging identity in coordinate $j$. Finally, the discrete product rule pulls out $D_jg_i(x)$ because $D_i g_j$ is independent of coordinate $j$ ($\texttt{FABL.discreteDerivative\_mul\_of\_right\_independent}$). This leaves the claimed expected product $D_jg_i\,D_ig_j$. The independence of each thresholded derivative from its own coordinate is supplied by $\texttt{FABL.isCoordinateIndependent\_polynomialDerivativeThreshold}$.
