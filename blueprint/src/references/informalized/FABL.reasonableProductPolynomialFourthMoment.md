# Fourth Moment Bound for Product Polynomials

**Claim.** For independent finite-coordinate random variables with mean and third moment zero, if each coordinate is reasonable with constant $B$ and the multilinear polynomial has degree at most $k$, its fourth moment is at most $\max(B,9)^k$ times the square of its second moment.

**Proof.** Proceed by induction on the number of coordinates. With no coordinates, the polynomial is the constant coefficient; the expectation of a constant is that constant, and $\max(B,9)^k\ge 1$, giving the asserted inequality. In positive dimension, first handle $k=0$: the degree-zero polynomial is constant, so the same constant calculation proves the claim. For positive degree, write the polynomial as $X_0d+e$, where $d$ is the odd part and $e$ the even part in the first coordinate; both depend only on the remaining coordinates. The degree bounds for these coefficient families are respectively at most $k-1$ and at most $k$ (\texttt{FABL.multilinearOddCoefficients_degreeAtMost} and \texttt{FABL.multilinearEvenCoefficients_degreeAtMost}). Set $M=\max(B,9)$, and denote the first-coordinate second and fourth moments by $m_2,m_4$, the tail moments of $d,e$ by $D_2,D_4,E_2,E_4$, and the tail expectation of $d^2e^2$ by $C$. These quantities are nonnegative because they are expectations of nonnegative functions (\texttt{FABL.pmfExpectation_nonneg}). The induction hypothesis gives $D_4\le M^{k-1}D_2^2$ and $E_4\le M^kE_2^2$; coordinate reasonableness gives $m_4\le M m_2^2$. The moment decompositions in the first coordinate give $\mathbb E[f^2]=m_2D_2+E_2$ and $\mathbb E[f^4]=m_4D_4+6m_2C+E_4$ (\texttt{FABL.productMultilinearRandomVariable_secondMoment_cons} and \texttt{FABL.productMultilinearRandomVariable_fourthMoment_cons}). For the mixed term, Cauchy–Schwarz in the tail law gives $C^2\le D_4E_4$ (\texttt{FABL.pmfExpectation_sq_mul_sq_sq_le}). Combining this with the induction bounds and $M\ge9$ yields $C\le (M^k/3)D_2E_2$. Therefore $m_4D_4\le M^k(m_2D_2)^2$, $6m_2C\le2M^k(m_2D_2)E_2$, and $E_4\le M^kE_2^2$. Adding these inequalities gives $\mathbb E[f^4]\le M^k(m_2D_2+E_2)^2$, as required.
